# Radiological Risk Evaluation of Fly Ash Utilization in Asphalt Pavements

## Overview

This project investigates the use of **fly ash as a partial replacement in BC-2 bituminous pavement mixtures**, focusing on both mechanical performance and radiological safety. Fly ash was incorporated at different replacement levels and compared with a conventional asphalt mix. The study combines **Marshall stability testing, gamma spectrometry, radiological hazard assessment, and SEM analysis** to evaluate the suitability of fly ash for pavement applications.

## Objectives

* To evaluate the **Marshall stability and flow characteristics** of asphalt mixtures containing fly ash.
* To determine the concentrations of **Ra-226, Th-232, and K-40** using gamma spectrometry.
* To assess radiological safety using **Radium Equivalent Activity (Raeq), External Hazard Index (Hex), Internal Hazard Index (Hin), and Outdoor Absorbed Dose Rate**.
* To investigate the **microstructure of the mixtures using SEM**.
* To assess the overall suitability of fly ash for use in **outdoor pavement construction**.

## Methodology

1. **Material Characterization** – Physical and engineering properties of aggregates and VG-30 bitumen were determined through standard laboratory tests.
2. **Mix Preparation** – BC-2 bituminous mixtures were prepared with conventional aggregate and fly ash replacement levels of **10%, 20%, and 30%**.
3. **Marshall Testing** – Marshall Stability and Flow tests were conducted to evaluate the mechanical performance of the prepared mixtures.
4. **Gamma Spectrometry** – Samples were analyzed using a **Canberra p-type HPGe detector** to determine the activity concentrations of Ra-226, Th-232, and K-40.
5. **Radiological Assessment** – Raeq, Hex, Hin, and outdoor absorbed dose rate were calculated to evaluate potential radiological risks.
6. **SEM Analysis** – Scanning Electron Microscopy was used to examine the microstructural characteristics of the mixtures.
